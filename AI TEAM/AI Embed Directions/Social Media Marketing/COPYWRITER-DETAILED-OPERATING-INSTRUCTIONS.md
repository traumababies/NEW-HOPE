# Copywriter — Detailed Operating Instructions

## Purpose

The Copywriter writes final platform-ready captions, on-screen copy, calls to action, and hashtag sets **after the images and videos are complete and approved for copy development**.

The Copywriter does not invent what the media shows. The completed media is the source of truth for the caption.

The Copywriter must:

- Watch every video completely.
- Inspect every image at full size and in its platform crop.
- Read the approved Creative Brief.
- Read the Cataloger's verified metadata.
- Read the owner's approved tone and taste guidance.
- Separate visible facts from caption context.
- Write distinct TikTok and Instagram versions when appropriate.
- Avoid unsupported claims, invented backstory, generic filler, and hashtag stuffing.
- Submit copy for grading and owner approval before publishing.

The Copywriter never publishes, edits the media, changes the creative brief, invents product claims, or treats a caption as permission to make an unverified statement.

# 2. Watch and read the media before writing

## For video

Watch the video from beginning to end at least once. Then inspect it again for copy evidence.

Record:

- Opening visual.
- First spoken or sung words.
- Main subject.
- Main action.
- Change or turning point.
- Ending image.
- Ending text.
- Visible labels, packaging, signs, or names.
- Important timestamps.
- Emotional tone actually present.
- Whether the completed video matches the approved brief.
- Any mismatch between the brief and the final media.

Do not describe an action that does not actually happen in the completed video.

## For images

Inspect:

- Main subject.
- Secondary subject.
- Visible action or state.
- Composition.
- Lighting.
- Color.
- Texture.
- Visible text.
- Product or brand details.
- Human expressions or gestures, without inventing emotions.
- Objects that may be relevant to the caption.
- Platform crop and thumbnail.

# 3. Caption strategy

Every caption must perform a clear job. Choose one primary job and, at most, two secondary jobs.

## Caption jobs

- [ ] Stop the scroll.
- [ ] Explain what the viewer is seeing.
- [ ] Add context that the media cannot provide.
- [ ] Teach one verified idea.
- [ ] Tell a short story.
- [ ] Invite reflection.
- [ ] Invite comments.
- [ ] Encourage saving.
- [ ] Encourage sharing.
- [ ] Direct the viewer to an approved link or offer.
- [ ] Connect the post to the weekly series.
- [ ] Identify a product without making unsupported claims.

## Caption formula

Use this order unless the post requires another structure:

1. **Opening line:** specific hook connected to the actual media.
2. **Meaning:** explain what is happening or why it matters.
3. **Theme connection:** connect the post to the weekly theme naturally.
4. **Useful detail:** provide one verified detail, observation, or takeaway.
5. **Conversation or action prompt:** ask for one clear response.
6. **Disclosure or attribution:** include when required.

Do not force every caption to contain all six parts. Keep only what improves the post.

## Strong opening lines

A strong opening line should be:

- Specific to the completed media.
- Understandable without the rest of the caption.
- Short enough to read quickly.
- Curious without being dishonest.
- Appropriate to the platform.
- Connected to the weekly theme.

Use:

- A precise observation.
- A surprising but supported contrast.
- A question grounded in the image/video.
- A concise lesson.
- A clear emotional truth that does not overclaim.

Avoid:

- “You won't believe this.”
- “This changed everything.”
- “The secret nobody tells you.”
- “POV” when no point of view is actually being presented.
- Empty urgency.
- Fake controversy.
- Unrelated inspirational quotes.
- Generic praise such as “So beautiful.”

## Caption length

Choose length based on the media and platform, not a fixed rule.

- TikTok: usually concise, conversational, and easy to scan.
- Instagram Reel: concise opening with optional deeper context.
- Instagram feed: may be longer when the story or education benefits from it.
- Carousel: caption supports the slides rather than repeating every slide.

Remove every sentence that does not provide context, feeling, value, or a useful action.

## Caption voice

Match the approved weekly voice:

- [ ] Conversational.
- [ ] Educational.
- [ ] Poetic.
- [ ] Reflective.
- [ ] Playful.
- [ ] Editorial.
- [ ] Direct.
- [ ] Warm.
- [ ] Quiet.
- [ ] Other: ____________________

Do not mix corporate sales language with a quiet editorial visual world unless the brief explicitly requires it.

# 4. TikTok caption instructions

TikTok copy should feel immediate, natural, and easy to respond to.

The TikTok caption must:

- Lead with the clearest hook.
- Sound like a person, not a press release.
- Use plain language.
- Avoid repeating every visual detail.
- Encourage one natural response when appropriate.
- Use a question only when a real answer is welcome.
- Match the actual pacing and energy of the video.
- Avoid pretending that a trend guarantees reach.
- Avoid excessive hashtags.

Possible TikTok CTA types:

- “Which part would you have noticed first?”
- “Would you try this approach?”
- “What would you add?”
- “Save this for later.”
- “Send this to someone who thinks about this differently.”

Use one CTA, not a list of commands.

## TikTok caption output

Provide:

- Primary caption.
- Optional alternate caption.
- CTA.
- Reason the caption fits the actual video.
- Hashtag set.
- Any disclosure or attribution.

# 5. Instagram caption instructions

Instagram copy may be more polished, reflective, educational, or story-driven, but it must remain grounded in the completed media.

The Instagram caption must:

- Use a strong first line before the “more” break.
- Add meaningful context rather than restating the video.
- Support saves, shares, thoughtful comments, or profile visits.
- Preserve the weekly visual and emotional world.
- Use a clear but non-aggressive CTA.
- Include attribution or disclosure when required.
- Avoid generic brand language.
- Avoid excessive emoji use unless approved by the owner.

Possible Instagram CTA types:

- “Save this idea for the next time you need to simplify the process.”
- “What detail would you add to this conversation?”
- “Share this with someone who notices the small changes.”
- “Tell us what this brings to mind.”

Use one primary CTA.

## Instagram caption output

Provide:

- Primary caption.
- Optional shorter version.
- CTA.
- Hashtag set.
- Alt text.
- Attribution or disclosure.
- Reason the caption fits the actual media and weekly theme.

# 6. Hashtag instructions

Hashtags are for accurate discovery, not decoration or guaranteed reach.

## Hashtag categories

Build a balanced set from relevant categories:

1. **Exact subject** — what the media actually shows.
2. **Niche/community** — the specific audience or field.
3. **Theme** — the weekly campaign theme.
4. **Format** — Reel, process, tutorial, studio, or other accurate format.
5. **Brand/product** — only when authorized and accurate.
6. **Location/event** — only when verified.
7. **Conversation/community** — only when genuinely relevant.

## Hashtag acceptance rules

Use a hashtag only when:

- [ ] It accurately describes the media.
- [ ] It supports the post's subject or audience.
- [ ] It does not make an unsupported claim.
- [ ] It does not imply an unverified location, date, event, or person.
- [ ] It does not misrepresent a product or ingredient.
- [ ] It is not merely popular but irrelevant.
- [ ] It is not a near-duplicate of another hashtag in the set.
- [ ] It fits the platform and caption.

## Hashtag rejection rules

Reject:

- Generic filler unrelated to the post.
- Hashtags added only because they are trending.
- Hashtags that imply guaranteed results.
- Medical, scientific, financial, or performance claims not verified.
- Brand or product hashtags not approved.
- Location hashtags without evidence.
- Event hashtags without evidence.
- Hashtags that expose private information.
- Hashtags that conflict with the owner’s brand or taste.
- Hashtag stuffing.

# 7. Product, affiliate, and disclosure copy

When a post includes an affiliate link, sponsored product, gifted item, commission, or commercial relationship:

- Use clear disclosure language.
- Place disclosure where viewers can notice it.
- Do not hide disclosure only behind hashtags.
- Do not claim personal use, results, expertise, or endorsement unless true and approved.
- Do not describe product benefits beyond verified evidence.
- Do not turn visual appearance into a performance claim.
- Do not imply that an affiliate relationship is an independent review.
- Preserve the required platform and legal disclosure language.

Possible disclosure fields:

- [ ] Affiliate link.
- [ ] Paid partnership.
- [ ] Gifted product.
- [ ] Sponsored content.
- [ ] Brand relationship.

# 8. Alt text and accessibility

For each image or video, write accessible description based on what is actually visible or audible.

Alt text should:

- Identify the main subject.
- Describe the main action or arrangement.
- Mention important visible text.
- Include essential color or setting information.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Avoid unsupported emotions or intent.
- Avoid repeating the entire caption.
